## Platform

## Users

- **Primary: the owner / delivery lead** (the person who commissioned Odin). Opens it daily to answer one question first: *where is every deployment right now, and what is stuck?* Self-described "visual" — wants to see state, not read it. Then drills into one customer's rollout.
- **Delivery / project managers (Member role):** run each rollout day to day — move stages, tick tasks, upload deliverables, write status notes.
- **Sales / account managers (Contributor role):** create a project from a customer PO, watch progress, comment; never move a delivery stage.
- **Viewers:** read-only staff.
- **External participants (contractors, resellers, customer contacts):** invited to a project by email (only from allowed partner domains, if the admin set any), sign in with a one-time link (no password; opening it shows a Continue button so mail scanners can't spend it; lifetime set by the admin), see only the projects they are on, read and comment, tick tasks tagged to them. A customer contact may also approve or decline each stage change when the project asks for it.
- **Customers / vendors via the older per-project guest links** (customer: view and comment; vendor, on Outsourced rollouts only: also advance one step, never to the last stage): still live until invited accounts replace them. A link stops working after `guest_link_days` (default 180) from when it was made or last rotated (rotating renews it; 0 = never), and an admin switch retires them all.

## Product Purpose

Odin tracks the delivery lifecycle of every cybersecurity product and service the company sells — from purchase order through scoping, kickoff, deployment, tuning/UAT and hypercare to closed — for a security company running many rollouts in parallel. Success: nothing stalls unnoticed; the team, the customer and the vendor all see the same state of a rollout; the audit trail proves who did what.

Deliberately **not** a billing or commercial tool: invoices, payments and margins were removed and live elsewhere.

## Positioning

One shared pipeline for internal team, customer and vendor, with **stage SLAs measured from history** (time-in-stage reconstructed from status notes, targets set by hand or from measured averages), **parties with different powers** (staff by project role; partners and customers by emailed one-time link; the older anonymous guest links), a **plan, baseline and RAID register** that turn into an honest customer report, a **sign-off with e-signature** that gates closing a rollout, and an **append-only audit trail** built for ISO 27001 / SOC 2 evidence. **AI is local and only suggests:** drafts, proposals and explanations come from a model on the company's own network, are queued and never run in a web request, and land as suggestions a person accepts or edits through the normal audited route; Odin refuses an AI address outside the private network unless an admin allows it. Generic project tools do not know what a security-product rollout is; Odin's stages, sign-off and parties are that rollout.

## Operating Context

- Internal Docker deployment on Proxmox (web app, nginx and a separate scheduler service), published through a Cloudflare Tunnel; staff sign in with Microsoft (Entra ID) or a password with two-step sign-in, so Odin does not depend on Cloudflare Access (guest links and partner links would bypass it anyway). Production updates itself: a cron job on the server moves to the newest release tag on `main` (backup, rebuild, health and preflight checks, back to the previous tag if any step fails; `DEPLOY.md`). An AI server (alias P24-AI, an OpenAI-compatible API) sits on the office LAN, shared with the company's CRM and managed outside this repo.
- A rollout is created from a **client PO document** (required). Each has a client, vendor, product, delivery mode (Distribution/MSSP or Outsourced), owner, target end date, its own stage list, tasks with assignees and due dates, a plan and baseline, a RAID register, agreed scope, uploaded deliverables, status notes (some customer-visible), saved report versions, a sign-off, saved lessons, and two guest links (the vendor link only when Outsourced). Since v2.0.0 it also holds the workspace's custom fields, its own milestones, tasks that wait on other tasks, and the asks put to its customer and partners. A rollout may also arrive from the CRM as a pending intake, which a project manager confirms by attaching the PO.
- Stages are configurable per install; default lifecycle: PO Received → Scoping → Kickoff → Deployment → Tuning/UAT → Hypercare → Sign-off → Closed. The last stage is terminal everywhere, and it cannot be reached until the sign-off is complete (while `signoff_required` is on).
- **Alerts**, nine types, each with a severity, an in-app switch and its own email and Teams switches (Settings → Workflow): *overdue* (past target end), *due soon*, *stage SLA breached*, *idle* (untouched), and four bottleneck alerts with a wait in days: *customer approval waiting* (default 5), *blocker still open* (3), *overdue task with no owner* (1), *customer task overdue* (3), and *milestone missed* (a rollout's milestone passed undone; in the bell by default). They are raised once by the scheduler, listed on the Alerts page and the dashboard, reach the bell of the owner and the staff on the rollout (email and Teams per type), and clear when the cause does. A daily digest goes to Admins and to each project manager for their own rollouts; Teams posts for the channel when connected.
- Rituals: daily glance at the portfolio; moving a card when a milestone lands; sending a guest link to a customer/vendor; quarterly access review from the users export; nightly backup.
- Scale: dozens to a few hundred active rollouts; a team of roughly 20–50.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Stack: Flask + SQLite JSON API; React + TypeScript + shadcn/ui front end (Vite, Tailwind 4). Every signed-in page is React; only the public guest pages, the login fallback form and the error page remain Jinja. Recurring work (digest, backups, weekly report, retention, the AI worker, the outbox) runs only in the scheduler service, never in a web worker. Roles: Admin, Member, Contributor, Viewer and External; Admins alone act on an Admin's account or the role matrix.
- Roles/permissions matrix (Admin, Member, Contributor, Viewer, External) enforced server-side; the UI must reflect them (Viewers and Externals see, never move). Per project, every participant also has a project role (Project manager = owner, Sales, Coordinator, Contractor, Reseller, Customer), and it binds staff too: only the Project manager moves stages, edits the project, its stage list, team, report and guest links; a Coordinator runs tasks and documents; everyone else on or off the project reads, comments, and ticks tasks tagged to them; a Customer contact approves stage changes. Admins bypass the project role. Since v2.0.0: moving past a stage gate needs an Admin, or the project manager while `gate_override` allows it, always with a typed reason; automations are written by Admins alone (`manage_rules`); confirming a CRM intake needs `create_project` (and a project manager who can move stages); Workload, People and the calendar feed are staff only; following a rollout is for staff, muting it for anyone on it; outside parties see and answer only their own asks, never an internal task, a gate, a rating or an automation.
- Every project owns its stage list: copied from a template in Settings at creation, then editable per project (add, rename, reorder, delete, SLAs). There is no shared stage header; each row carries its stage by name.
- Tasks belong to a stage of their project and are worked from the flow screen: add with an assignee from the project team, tick, delete (with Undo, which also gives back the links of the tasks that waited on it). The person a task is tagged to may tick it whatever their role. Since v2.0.0 a task may **wait on** other tasks of the same rollout (finish-to-start, never a loop; it cannot be ticked while they are open, an Admin may on the record; the plan's forecast carries a late predecessor's slip down the chain of tasks that wait on it: a carry-down, not a critical path, which Odin does not compute), carry an **estimate in hours** (for Workload), and **repeat** weekly, fortnightly or monthly until a day or until its stage is done (the next one is made once, when it is ticked or its day passes, and waits on what this one waited on; since a repeating task always has an occurrence open, it holds a stage gate and counts as open in its stage only while that occurrence is overdue, 17 Oct 2026). A template's stages can bring **template tasks** (a title, the project role it lands on, a due offset in days from the stage's start, an estimate and one task it waits on), copied as real tasks when a rollout is made from it. **Quick add** on My work puts a task on one of my rollouts without opening it. The RAID register and the tasks export to CSV (staff, audited).
- **Plan and baseline** (30 Sep 2026): planned start/end per stage on the project page (Plan panel), with "fill the gaps from SLAs" and cascading moves. The first save freezes the plan as the baseline; every later move is logged with its reason, and re-baselining needs one. The forecast chains done stages (actual dates), the current stage (at least its planned length, never before today) and the rest (plan length, else SLA, else `default_stage_days`). Stage lengths, SLAs and time in a stage count **working days** (the working week minus the public holidays in Settings → General; `count_working_days` switches back to calendar days), while gaps between dates (slip, days late, days to target) stay calendar days; no digest or reminders go out on a holiday. Health is stored as red / amber / green and **read as On track / At risk / Off track** (and Complete), always with its reasons and a confidence word; the thresholds are policies (`rag_amber_days`, `rag_red_days`); open blockers turn a rollout red, high-impact risks amber. The customer copy shows the word, never the internal reasons.
- **RAID register**: risks, issues, decisions and blockers per rollout, with owner (notified), due date, stage, impact/likelihood, resolution, and a "show in the customer's report" flag.
- **Custom fields** (v2.0.0, Settings › Rollouts & workflow): up to `custom_fields_max` (20) extra fields per rollout — text, number, date, choice or yes-no — each optionally required at creation, shown to the customer and partners (their project page, the customer report, `{field:Name}` in documents) and shown as a column on the rollouts list. The CRM intake and automations can set them; the API returns them.
- **Stage gates** (v2.0.0): a stage, on a template and per rollout, may ask that all its tasks are done and that documents of named categories are on file before the rollout leaves it. Every move path checks it (the flow, the Kanban, the API, the vendor link); only a move forward is gated and a jump answers for every stage it passes. An Admin, or the project manager while `gate_override` says `admin_and_pm`, moves past it with a typed reason: audited, and kept in the stage history as a staff note. Staff see what holds the move on the flow row, the rollout and the internal report; a gate due to be left is a health reason. The customer and the vendor link hear only that the move waits on the team.
- **Milestones and repeating work** (v2.0.0): a milestone is a dated commitment of a rollout (a name, a day, optionally its stage, and whether the customer sees it), marked reached by hand. Milestones show on the Calendar, the dashboard timeline, both report cuts and the portfolio's next-milestone column, and in the calendar feed; one passing undone raises *milestone missed*. A milestone kept for the team is never named in the customer's words.
- **Rollout report** (`/projects/<id>/report`, 30 Sep 2026): one page and one PDF in two cuts. Since v2.0.0 both cuts carry the custom fields and milestones (the customer's: those marked for them); the internal one also says what holds the next move (the gate) and lists what was asked of the customer and partners, what came back, and their rating at sign-off (a section of its own in the document template). Internal: health and reasons, forecast vs target and baseline, Gantt (plan, actual, forecast, baseline), stage table with slip, what's next, the whole RAID register, work by stage with a burn-up, people, history (dwell vs SLA, approvals, plan changes, notes, documents). Customer copy: no baseline, slip, reasons, people or internal notes; only customer-marked RAID items and notes; plus "what we need from you" (pending approval, customer tasks, the PM's asks). The words (summary, next steps, asks) are drafted from the numbers and edited by the PM; "Save as version" freezes data and words as a numbered snapshot. Partners and customers see saved versions only, cut to the customer copy. Old plain-text versions still read.
- **Dashboard customisation** (30 Sep 2026): no edit mode; every widget has its own ⋯ menu (settings, width, earlier/later, remove), each change saves at once with an Undo; "Add widget" and a layout menu (admin: save as workspace default; reset). Rows never leave a gap: widgets pack in order, a row with room pulls up the next widget that fits and stretches its last one, for three, two or one columns.
- **Last seen and time on Odin**: active minutes are counted while Odin is open, visible and in use (a ping a minute, idle after `activity_idle_minutes`, at most one minute per 50 s); People shows last seen; a person's page shows today / 7 / 30 days and a 30-day strip to the roles in `activity_roles` (default Admins and Members); Settings → Sign-up & activity can switch it off (`activity_tracking`: nothing counted, panels gone, last seen = last sign-in); everyone sees their own on the Account page, with a note on what is measured.
- **Sign-up links**: admins make links (who for, role Member/Contributor/Viewer, optional project and project role, days (default `signup_days`), uses; revocable one by one or all at once; `signup_enabled` off stops every link and the making of new ones). A new colleague fills /join/<token> (name, work email within `signup_domains`, username, password) and waits as a pending account; admins are notified and approve on the Users page, which activates them and adds them to the project. How a person is told is not chosen per person: every notification lands in the bell, and the "Tell me about" switches on the Account page decide by topic which ones also go by email and, for staff, to their Teams chat.
- **Document templates** (Settings → Documents): templates for the rollout report, the portfolio report and the sign-off — title, accent, our logo, an optional customer co-brand logo, a company line, a footer, sections on/off and in order, and text blocks with placeholders ({client}, {project}, {company}, {pm}, {po}, {stage}, {target}, {forecast}, {date}); one default per kind; the writer picks another per document; a preview renders the unsaved template on the newest project. Issued documents keep their template (a sign-off inside its fingerprint; a saved report version in its snapshot).
- **Scope and sign-off** (30 Sep 2026, the last phase of every rollout): the agreed scope is a list on the project page (Scope and sign-off panel), recorded while scoping and marked delivered / partly delivered / descoped at the end (the last two need a reason). A **Sign-off** stage sits before the terminal stage in every list (added to existing templates and open projects once; new stages are inserted before it), and the terminal stage cannot be reached until the sign-off is complete while `signoff_required` is on. The sign-off page (`/projects/<id>/signoff`) drafts the document — the scope item by item, or, with no scope, what was delivered from the stages and deliverables; outstanding items; handover and support terms (policy `signoff_terms`); the acceptance statement (policy `signoff_statement`) — and the project manager edits and sends it to a Customer or Reseller contact on the project, naming who countersigns for us. Sending freezes the version as canonical JSON with a SHA-256 fingerprint. The signatory opens it from the email's one-time sign-in link, reviews, and signs (drawn or typed, with name, title and an accept box) or sends it back with comments, which become an open customer-visible issue; then we countersign. The PDF certificate carries both signatures and the signing record (fingerprint, integrity check, who, when, from where — IPs for staff only). Fallbacks: upload a signed paper copy against an open version (filed under the Sign-off document category), or an admin waives it with a written reason. Every step is audited and notified; the signed PDF is mailed to both sides when mail is on.
- **Portfolio report** (`/reports/all`, PDF at `/reports/all.pdf`): health mix, late against target, behind baseline, open blockers, finishing in 30 days, the average customer rating (internal), a roadmap bar per rollout (start to forecast, baseline finish and target marked, worst health first), the slippage league, blockers and high-impact risks, and every rollout with its next milestone (and how many it missed) and its latest report. Partners and customers get the customer cut and no PDF.
- **Notifications**: a stage change reaches every participant with an address (actor excluded), plus the owner; the project manager is also told when anyone else adds a note, finishes a task or uploads a document on their rollout; an approval decision reaches every participant; people added to a rollout or made its manager are told. Every notification lands in the header bell (unread count, mark read, click opens the rollout) whether or not mail is connected; the bell is written at once. **Email** (Microsoft 365, sent from the dedicated mailbox, optionally as an alias) and **Teams** go through an **outbox** the scheduler drains: a failed send is retried after 1, 2, 4, 8 minutes and so on, given up after `outbox_attempts`, and the admins hear of it once a day, so a Microsoft outage never slows or breaks a save. Teams has three forms: a **card in each staff member's own chat** from the "Odin PMS" Teams app (notification-only; partners and customers get email only), posts to a channel, and a Workflows webhook. Each user chooses by topic (stage changes, approval decisions, activity on rollouts they manage, tasks assigned, mentions, due reminders, notes from colleagues, alerts, the morning brief, milestones passing undone, reports shared with them, automations) whether it also comes by email and Teams **at once, in one daily summary** at the digest time, or not at all (v2.0.0); the bell is always at once. Anyone on a rollout may **mute** it (the bell keeps it, marked muted; no mail), and staff not on it may **follow** it (its stage moves, activity, alerts and milestones). A mail about a rollout ends with **the reader's next step** there (their three soonest open tasks; for a customer, what we need from them). **Chasers**: a sign-off out for signature and a stage approval waiting on the customer are reminded after `chase_after_days` working days and again every as many days, at most `chase_max` times (0 switches it off); the project manager sees the count; admins also get sign-in requests, AI server notices, and a daily notice of failed jobs, overdue backups and messages Odin could not send. Mails link back into Odin (an external account through a fresh one-time sign-in link); the rollout's Activity feed shows the audit trail's own rows for people who do not read their email. A note can mention a team member as "@Full Name", "@username" or a unique "@First"; only people on the project can be mentioned. Nobody is told about their own action, and nobody gets the same event twice. Each morning the daily script reminds every assignee of their tasks due today or overdue, in one summary; no reminder goes out on a holiday. **Sign-in mail** (sign-in links, password resets, address confirmations, invitations) can go out through **Resend** instead of Microsoft 365 (Settings › Notifications › Sign-in mail; 16 Oct 2026), falling back to Microsoft 365 when Resend refuses; everything else stays on Microsoft 365. Staff who forget their password request a one-time, 30-minute reset link from the sign-in page (the reply never says whether the address exists; a reset signs the account out everywhere). Optional per rollout: the customer contact is asked to approve each stage change; the stage still moves, the decision is recorded, noted and audited. The vendor link moves stages through the normal path (approval, history, the team told).
- Guest pages are public-internet surfaces: no internal navigation, no internal notes, no PO number on the vendor link, author name required on every write, writes rate-limited (`guest_writes_per_minute`), and the link expires (see Users).
- **Deleting a signed-off rollout** (29 Oct 2026, the user's call): it needs a reason (10 to 1,000 characters), the admins get a bell item, the audit row carries the reason, and the signed record (PDF, a JSON with the frozen content and signature columns, the customer's rating, the paper copy if any) is written to `signoff-archive/` before the rollout goes. It stays after the nightly purge for the audit retention period (`audit_keep`), is in every backup and is listed under Settings → Audit → Archives. Undo restores the rollout and leaves the files where they are.
- Drag-and-drop stage changes exist and are audited; vendor may advance one step only, never to terminal. The person who moved a stage can undo it for five minutes while it is still the latest move; the undo is audited and the team is told.
- All stored timestamps are UTC; rendered in the reader's zone.
- **Staff sign in with Microsoft** (Entra ID, one tenant, its own app registration). Microsoft proves identity; Odin decides the role. A colleague unknown to Odin waits for an admin to approve them with a role. Accounts are bound by Microsoft's object id; the email links an existing account on first sign-in only when its owner proved the address by opening a link mailed to it and the account has no usable password. A colleague with a password signs in with it and uses **Account → Link Microsoft**; a first Microsoft sign-in never takes such an account over. Admins can require Microsoft for all staff; accounts they flag keep an emergency password form at /login/password, every use audited and announced to admins. Partners and customers never use Microsoft: they sign in with the emailed one-time link.
- **System map & changes** (Settings → Compliance, admins): the Odin System Map inside the app — architecture, lifecycle, notifications, permissions, ways in, code structure, tests, deployment, the loophole register — and a change log of every version, grouped by area, with the changes not yet released under the next version. Generated from the repository and shipped with each release, so it always describes the code the server runs.
- **Hardening in 1.3.0**: signing out ends that session server-side (a copied cookie stops working); Microsoft 365 tokens and client secrets are sealed at rest; access logs redact secret links; a failed restore test prunes no backup anywhere; settings routes follow the permission matrix; API token reads are audited daily; email failures while email is on reach the admins' bell.
- **Two-step sign-in** (1 Oct 2026) for password accounts: an authenticator-app code (TOTP) after the password, or one of ten single-use recovery codes. Anyone turns it on from the Account page (QR code or typed key, then a code; recovery codes shown once, downloadable); turning it off or making new codes needs the password and a code. The admin may require it for admins or everyone (`mfa_required`); someone who must and has not set it up is kept on the Account page until they do. Admins reset it for a lost phone (Users page; their sessions end). Microsoft sign-in keeps Microsoft's own check; partners' email links are unchanged. Secrets are sealed with a key derived from SECRET_KEY; codes work once.
- **My work** (`/my-work`): headline numbers (open, overdue, done this week, my rollouts), a fortnight of what got done, tasks grouped by urgency — overdue, today, this week, later, undated — ticked off in place, and the rollouts the person is on with their role and state.
- **People** (`/people`, staff only): a card for everyone internal on active work (rollouts, open, late, done in 30 days). A person's page shows their KPIs (incl. on-time rate), contribution per week, active rollouts, pending and upcoming tasks, what they did recently, and **Notify** — a note that lands in their bell and inbox (`nudge` notification kind, audited), optionally about one rollout.
- **AI** (8 Oct 2026, Settings → AI): a framework in `services/ai/` that talks to one OpenAI-compatible server on the office LAN (alias P24-AI; address, model, identity line, key and schedule are all policies). **Data handling:** a rollout's notes, risks, scope, tasks and documents go only to that server, only for a feature an admin has switched on; an address outside the private network is refused unless `ai_allow_external` is on; input is trimmed to `ai_max_input_chars` (4,000); every request is logged (who, feature, size, time; not the text) and the log is kept `ai_keep_days`. **It never decides:** nothing is sent, moved or closed by a model; output is stored as a suggestion on the rollout that a person accepts, edits or dismisses through the normal audited route, and words the customer reads are saved by the project manager. **A feature runs only when AI is on, the server's heartbeat says it is up, the feature is on, and it passed validation** (a synthetic rollout with graders) on the current model and prompt; otherwise Odin uses the plain template or shows nothing. The scheduler's AI worker queues and runs everything (heartbeat every few minutes, validation at 03:00, the night shift at 03:30, the morning brief at 07:30); a web request only queues a job, and a draft can be cancelled. Fourteen features: *weekly words* (report summary, next steps, asks), *customer words* (tidy the words for the customer), *risks proposed* (from recent notes, each with the words that show it), *why is this amber / red* (two sentences and a first move), *portfolio narrative* (the weekly email's opening), *sign-off summary*, *SOW to plan* (the PO and scope document turned into suggested scope items, tasks, obligations and dates), *chase drafts* (a polite follow-up to the customer), *morning brief* (a short note per person), *catch me up* (a one-page brief for whoever takes a rollout over), *SLA calibration* (a sentence per proposal when an SLA disagrees with history), *ask this rollout* (answers cite the passages used), *meeting notes to tasks* and *lessons drafted*. Arithmetic is always in code.
- **Lessons** (8 Oct 2026): a project manager saves what went wrong (avoid) and what to repeat from a rollout, each with its evidence, optionally drafted by AI from the plan changes, stage overruns, risks, descoped scope and notes. Lessons outlive their rollout. A rollout page shows the most similar other rollouts (same product, template, vendor, client; plain matching, not AI) with their lessons and the risks that actually hit them, so a new rollout can log them on day one. Staff only.
- **Flow views** (`/board?view=`, 13 Oct 2026): the rollout **List** (default); a **Kanban** by stage, where a card drags only to its own next stage through the usual confirm and checks; a **Gantt** of every rollout opening into its stages' plan, actuals, forecast and baseline, where whoever may edit a plan drags or resizes planned dates (a reason once baselined); and a **Calendar** (Month, Week, Agenda; task due dates, targets, stage moves and customer decisions, with running rollouts as bars) where a task can be dragged to another day. A rollout opened from any view opens in the same pane below it. The Gantt prints on A4 landscape with its timeline fitted to the page width (v2.0.0). Single-key shortcuts (`/` search, `?` the shortcut sheet, 1–4 the views) can be switched off on the Account page.
- **Dashboard** (`/dashboard`, the home page — sign-in and the logo land there unless a person picks the flow on their Account page; partners always get the flow): live — the page polls a version number every 15 s and on focus and reloads when anything changed; a tile opens the rollouts behind its number; every widget expands to a full-size chart with its data as a table; chart type is a per-widget setting; **custom metrics** (rollouts by stage / client / state / owner / mode, tasks by person, tasks per day — as a number, bars, a pie or a list) can be added any number of times; twelve built-in widgets on a grid — needs attention (every rollout in trouble, worst first, with its reasons), headline numbers with week-over-week deltas and 14-day trends (from a nightly snapshot), the portfolio timeline with teams, portfolio progress, schedule (due tasks and approvals waiting), tasks completed, team load, rollouts per stage, recent activity, open alerts, my work, cycle time — plus the custom metric. An admin arranges the workspace default on the page itself; each person can reorder, resize, hide and tune their own copy and reset to the default. Every number is one the flow, the alerts, the analytics or the weekly report already compute.
- **Weekly report** (Settings → Reports): emailed on the chosen days to staff in the chosen roles plus extra addresses — needs attention first, stages moved this week, due in the next 7 days, milestones missed or due this week, who is over capacity this week or next, rollouts per stage — optionally with the portfolio report and each active project's latest saved report as PDFs, and a summary to Teams (channel and/or a Workflows webhook, which also carries stage changes and decisions).
- **Audit retention** (Settings → Retention): 1–7 years or forever (default 3); older rows are archived nightly to a CSV (with a checksum, included in backups) before removal, and the removal itself is audited. The trail stays append-only for every account. The same page sets how long read notifications (`notifications_keep_days`, default 90; unread ones stay) and time on Odin (`activity_keep_days`, default 400) are kept.
- **Read-only API tokens** (Settings → API tokens): named, 30/90/365 days, shown once, stored only as a hash, revocable, last use shown, one audit row per token per day; `/api/v1` reads projects (with their custom fields, paged by `limit` and `offset` when asked), stages, tasks with what each waits on, milestones, asks and ratings, and the latest saved report, and nothing else. A token sees what internal staff see. Reference: `docs/API_V1.md`.
- **CRM intake** (v2.0.0, Settings › Integrations › Intake): the company's own CRM posts a won deal to `POST /api/v1/intake` with a token of the `create` scope (which opens nothing else). Odin makes a **pending** rollout: in the Flow's holding lane, the rollouts list's "Pending intake" view and a banner on its own page, and in no count, alert, dashboard figure or report. A project manager confirms it by attaching the client's PO and taking ownership (or discards it); it then starts as any new rollout, with the template's tasks. The deal id makes a retry safe (the same rollout, never a second); every field is typed and capped, contacts outside the partner domains are dropped, each token is rate-limited (`intake_rate_per_min`) and may be limited to named sources; every call is logged and every rollout made is audited. No vendor connector: the CRM is in-house.
- **Customer self-service** (v2.0.0): a task assigned to a customer, reseller or contractor on the rollout is an **ask**, and a task may be a plain tick, an **upload** (done when a document of the asked category arrives for it) or a short **form** (at most `ask_form_max_questions`, answered and checked question by question). An outside party sees "What we need from you" first on their rollout, with their own asks only; staff see every ask with what came back. When the customer signs the sign-off they may **rate the rollout** (1 to 5, an optional comment, once per version); ratings are internal (People, the internal report, the portfolio). Their writes are rate-limited like the guest pages. **A file an outside party uploads is held for review** (17 Oct 2026, the user's call; no antivirus): the ask counts as answered, but nobody can open or download the file, and it is kept out of partners' document list, reports, the sign-off, stage gates, search, AI and the OneDrive mirror, until the project manager (or anyone who manages the rollout's documents) **accepts** it under Documents; **reject** deletes it, reopens the ask and tells the partner. Both audited; the project manager's notice says "uploaded a file for review"; staff uploads are not held. A partner's task that waits on the team's work shows as any other: they never see the team's tasks or that one holds theirs, and only a tick of it is refused ("This waits on work the team has not finished yet"). Guide for customers: `docs/CUSTOMER_GUIDE.md`.
- **Automations** (v2.0.0, Settings › Automations, Admins): a rule is a trigger (a rollout created, which for a CRM intake means confirmed, a stage entered, an SLA breached, a task overdue, an ask completed, a document uploaded, a field set, the customer declining a stage, a milestone missed), conditions that must all hold (stage, delivery mode, field value, client) and actions: tell people (by name or project role, staff only), add a task from a template task, add a RAID item, set a field, post to Teams. **Rules reach staff and Teams only, never the customer, and never move a stage.** A rule's own actions start no other rule; conditions the alert pass finds fire once per rule and only for what arose after the rule was made or resumed; every firing is logged on the rule (kept `rule_runs_keep_days`) and audited; at most `rules_max` rules.
- **Workload** (v2.0.0, People › Workload, staff): hours per person per week, four weeks ahead: each open task due that week at its estimate (else `default_task_hours`), late work in the current week, plus `pm_hours_per_rollout` for every active rollout they manage, against their capacity (`capacity_hours_per_week`, 30, or their own on the Users page); amber from `workload_warn_pct`, red over 100 %. The task picker shows each person's hours that week. Hours and counts only, never money. **Hand-over**: deactivating someone can hand their open tasks, managed rollouts and RAID items to a colleague who can manage rollouts, in one audited step, and that colleague is told.
- **Calendar feed** (v2.0.0, Account page, staff): a personal ICS address for Outlook or any calendar app with one's open tasks with a due date and the targets and milestones of one's open rollouts, as all-day events. The address is shown once (only its hash is kept), can be rotated or switched off, and is rate-limited (`ics_feed_per_hour`). Partners and customers get none.
- **Since you last looked, and what's new** (v2.0.0): the dashboard says what changed on a person's rollouts since their previous visit (their own acts left out); after a release, staff see a short "What's new" from the change log once, until they dismiss it.
- **Access review** (v2.0.0, Users page): accounts worth a second look are flagged with the reason — no sign-in for `access_review_days` (90), or a partner whose rollouts have all closed — with one click to manage or deactivate (and hand over). A declined sign-up request keeps its one-line reason.
- **Updating** (30 Sep 2026): a release is a pull request from `dev` to `main` with `VERSION` raised; CI tags the merge `v<VERSION>`; the production server fetches `main` every 5 minutes and moves to the newest tag, backing up first and going back to the previous tag if the rebuild, the health checks or `preflight.py` fail. A failed tag is retried after 30 and 60 minutes, at most three times; admins see the failure in the bell. Updates can be paused by hand. The change log inside Odin is generated from git, one entry per tag.
- **Backups** run on the schedule set in Settings (scheduler service, workspace time zone): database snapshot + documents + pictures + manifest, restore-tested (integrity check, every document present), copied to OneDrive, a SharePoint site folder and/or a mounted network share, pruned by daily/weekly/monthly retention locally and at each destination; every run and its restore-test result is listed in Settings → Backups.
- Admin settings are grouped by area (General, Sign-in & security, People incl. Sign-up & activity, Rollouts & workflow, Notifications, Integrations, Reports, Backups, API tokens, Audit & retention, AI). Workspace policy an admin controls: company name, time zone, date format, working week and public holidays, new-user theme/accent, session idle and maximum length, password minimum, two-step sign-in requirement, sign-in attempt limit, upload size and types, undo window, default stage template and customer-approval default; since v2.0.0 also the custom-field and automation limits, who may move past a gate, the longest customer form, workload capacity and allowances, chasers, the calendar feed's rate, the intake defaults, the mail, Teams and OneDrive switches (policies now, no longer loose settings) and sign-in mail through Resend. Every change is validated against its limits and audited.
- Terminology (12 Oct 2026, the user's call): the UI says **rollout** wherever a person reads about the work (nav "Rollouts", "Open rollout", "the rollout can now close", emails and alerts); **project** stays only for the record and its form (New / Edit / Duplicate / Create / Delete project, "Project name", the project form), for the "Project manager" role, and in the brand tagline "Every project. On watch." URLs, API fields and template tokens (`{project}`) keep "project". Health reads in plan words only: On track / At risk / Off track, with the reason as detail. Also: *stage*; *task*; *deliverable*; *guest link* (customer / vendor); *SLA* = expected days in a stage.

## Product Principles

1. **State before text.** The first screen shows where every rollout is and what is stuck, without reading a table.
2. **One pipeline, three audiences.** Team, customer and vendor look at the same flow with different powers; the UI never shows a party more than it may do.
3. **Time is a first-class fact.** How long a stage has taken, against its SLA, is always visible where the stage is.
4. **Every move is evidence.** Anything that changes a rollout is attributed, audited, and reversible where the product allows.
5. **Density serves scanning.** Hundreds of rollouts must stay legible; cap, cluster and summarise rather than list.
