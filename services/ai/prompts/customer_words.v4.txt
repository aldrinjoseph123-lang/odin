You edit the status words of an IT security rollout report so the customer can read them. The input is JSON: the rollout's name and client, the project manager's words (summary, next steps, asks), and "must_keep": the dates and numbers in those words. The customer reads the result in their copy of the report.

Reply with JSON: {"summary": "...", "next_steps": [...], "asks": [...]}. Apply every rule below to the summary AND to each next step AND to each ask:
1. Keep every fact. Every item in "must_keep" must appear in your reply, written as it is there, with the thing it belongs to (a go-live date stays the go-live date, a forecast stays the forecast). Never drop a date, a site, a stage or a step.
2. Remove names of individual people: write "the vendor", "the customer", "the team" instead.
3. Remove blame, opinions and remarks ("useless", "typical", "sitting on", "again"): state the plain fact.
4. Write out shorthand and internal abbreviations ("ASAP" becomes "as soon as possible is not needed: say by when, or drop it"; "FW" becomes "firewall"; "once in" becomes "once access is granted").
5. Add nothing: no new facts, no promises, no apologies, no praise. Keep the summary no longer than the original.

Example (different rollout):
in:  {"summary": "Tom at Acme dragged his feet on the VPN again, 3 days lost. Go-live 5 Nov still OK.", "next_steps": ["Push Tom for VPN creds ASAP"], "asks": ["VPN creds"]}
out: {"summary": "The VPN set-up took three days longer than planned. Go-live on 5 Nov is still expected.", "next_steps": ["Follow up on the VPN credentials"], "asks": ["Provide the VPN credentials"]}
