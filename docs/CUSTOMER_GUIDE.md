# Odin for customers and partners

A short guide for the people outside our company who work on a rollout with us: the customer's
contacts, resellers and contractors. Send it with the first invitation. It describes Odin 2.0.

## Signing in

You do not need a password. We invite you by email to the rollouts you work on; the email carries a
sign-in link. Open it and press **Continue** (the button keeps mail scanners from using your link before
you do). Each link works once and for a limited time; when it has run out, ask the project manager for a
new one, or open the link in a later Odin email: every email we send you carries a fresh one.

Odin shows you only the rollouts you are on, and on each only what is meant for you.

## What you see on a rollout

- **What we need from you**, at the top: the things waiting on you (below). When nothing is, it says so.
- **Where the rollout stands**: its stages from the purchase order to closing, the stage it is in now, and
  whether it is on track. The dates are the plan and the forecast for what is left.
- **Milestones** we have shared with you, with their dates.
- **Notes** the team marked for you, and your own. You can add a note at any time; the project manager
  reads it, and may reply in a note of their own.
- **Documents** you may read and download (deliverables, the signed sign-off).
- **Reports**: the versions of the rollout report the project manager saved for you, as a page and as a PDF.

You do not see the team's internal notes, our internal health reasons, other parties' asks, or how we
plan our people.

## What we need from you: your asks

Each ask is one of three kinds, and you finish it where it is shown:

| Kind | What you do | When it is done |
|---|---|---|
| A task | Tick it when you have done it (e.g. "Confirm the cut-over change window") | When you tick it |
| An upload | Choose the file (e.g. "Upload the signed network diagram"); it is filed with the rollout's documents under the category we asked for | When the file is uploaded |
| A form | Answer a few short questions (a number, a date, a choice, yes or no, a line of text); Odin checks each answer and says what to fix | When every required question is answered |

An ask may have a due date. You see your own asks only; colleagues of yours on the rollout see theirs.

## Approving a stage

Some rollouts ask the customer to approve each stage change. When we move the rollout on, you get an email
and the rollout shows the decision waiting for you: **Approve** or **Decline**, with a comment saying why.
The rollout carries on either way; your decision is recorded with the stage and the team is told at once.

## Reminders

If a stage approval or the sign-off waits on you for a few working days, Odin sends a polite reminder, and
again a few days later, a limited number of times. When something waits on you, every email about the
rollout ends with what we need from you there, so you can act from the email.

## The sign-off

At the end, the project manager sends you the sign-off: what was delivered (item by item), anything
outstanding, the hand-over and support terms, and the statement you sign under. Open it from its email.

- **Sign** it by drawing or typing your signature, with your name and title and the acceptance box ticked.
  We then countersign, and both sides receive the signed PDF.
- Or write your comments and press **Send back**. They become an open point on the rollout, and we send a corrected
  version.

Each version is frozen when we send it, so what you sign is exactly what you read; the signed PDF carries
a fingerprint that proves it has not changed.

## Rating the rollout

When you sign, you may also rate the rollout from 1 (poor) to 5 (excellent), with a comment if you like.
It is optional, asked once per sign-off version, and read by our team only.

## Something not right?

Write a note on the rollout, or reply to the project manager. If a sign-in link does not work, it has
probably been used or has run out: any newer Odin email carries a fresh one.
