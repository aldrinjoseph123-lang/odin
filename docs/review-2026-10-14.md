# Odin full review, 14 Oct 2026 (senior pass)

Branch `dev` at 59d4d72 (VERSION 1.6.0; v1.7.0 is the pending release). Fourteen reviewers, a wave-2 verifier on every P0/P1, and this senior pass. Nothing in the code was changed. The source plan is `docs/review-plan-2026-10-14.md`.

How to read this report:
- **Grouped by fix, not by finder.** One row is one change a developer would make. "Angle" lists every reviewer who found part of it (numbers below). The best evidence is kept.
- **Effort:** S = under half a day, M = 1 to 3 days, L = more than 3 days.
- **Status:** VERIFIED = the wave-2 verifier reproduced or traced it; CONFIRMED = the reviewer ran it or read it end to end; SUSPECTED = inferred, not run. A group carries the weakest status of its parts unless its main part is stronger, which is said.
- **Order inside each table:** risk x effort, highest risk and lowest effort first.

Reviewers: 1 Backend logic, 2 API contract, 3 Security, 4 RBAC simulation, 5 Frontend code, 6 UI consistency, 7 UX and flows, 8 Visual checker, 9 Accessibility, 10 Tests and CI, 11 Data, migrations, ops, 12 Product fit, 13 Every button, 14 Best practices and quality of life (advisor).

## Summary

**No P0.** The permission layer holds under the default role matrix: no IDOR, no partner reaching another project, no SQL built from input, no unescaped HTML output (reviewers 3 and 4). The serious problems are one confidentiality leak on the sign-off, two broken promises in the UI (Undo, the one-time token), account-binding weaknesses around Microsoft sign-in, and an ops chain that can quietly stop purging, pruning and self-updating.

### The five worst

1. **P1-1 The sign-off page and PDF show the PO number, partner name and the customer signatory's email and signature to Contractors and Resellers**, while every other surface hides them (`core.sees_po`). A confidentiality rule the user already enforced everywhere else.
2. **P1-3 Deleting a project promises "five minutes to undo" and then gives no Undo and no message.** In practice a mistaken delete needs a database edit before the nightly purge.
3. **P1-2 A new API token is shown once, inside a 4-second toast with no pause and no copy button.** Every admin who makes a token meets it; the secret is also read aloud by the live region.
4. **P2-3 First Microsoft sign-in binds to any account with a matching, unverified email.** Someone with a sign-up link can pre-create "ceo@company" with their own password; after approval the real person's first Microsoft sign-in lands in that account and both can use it.
5. **P2-12 One upload file missing from disk makes every backup "fail", stops all pruning, and makes the production self-updater skip every release** ("backup failed: update skipped" every 5 minutes) until someone repairs the data. Disk then fills with unpruned zips. With P2-11 (purge stuck on a vendor FK) it is a quiet ops failure nobody is told about (P2-13).

## Best practices and quality of life (reviewer 14)

### Top ten, chosen by the senior pass from reviewer 14's ranking and its release order

1. **A1 A CI that runs:** a self-hosted runner in an LXC on Proxmox, or `scripts/ci_local.sh` as the fallback. Everything else depends on it.
2. **A3 Schema-version guard** with `PRAGMA user_version`, so older code cannot delete newer data on a rollback.
